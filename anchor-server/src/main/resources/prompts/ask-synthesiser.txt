How to populate `grounded_in_chapters` and `grounded_in_sections`:

- The bullet items in YOUR {Structural_Top_Plural} and YOUR RELEVANT
  {Structural_Mid_Plural} above are formatted as either
  `- "<title>": <summary>` or `- (unnamed segment): <summary>`.
- For each item you actually grounded in, copy the title VERBATIM from
  inside the double quotes — nothing before the `:`, nothing after,
  no summary text, no surrounding quotes themselves.
- SKIP items marked `(unnamed segment)`. Those are unnamed parts of
  the document with no title to cite — list nothing for them in the
  grounding arrays. The summary is real evidence; the section just
  has no name worth recording.
- Do NOT invent titles, paraphrase them, or concatenate
  `title: summary` together as a single "title".

Example. If the prompt above shows:

    YOUR CHAPTERS:
    - "1. Introduction": This chapter argues for the methodological...
    - (unnamed segment): A free-form summary of an unstructured...
    - "3. Main results": The results section reports counterexamples...

then a correctly-grounded `grounded_in_chapters` is:

    ["1. Introduction", "3. Main results"]

NOT any of these (all wrong):

    ["1. Introduction: This chapter argues for...", ...]    // copied summary
    ["(unnamed segment)", ...]                              // copied marker
    ["(unnamed segment): A free-form summary..."]           // copied bullet

If EVERY relevant item you grounded in is `(unnamed segment)`, the
correct value is the empty array `[]`. Do not invent a title to put
there, do not paraphrase a summary into a title, do not copy the
marker. The chapter-level array can carry the citation in that case;
unnamed sections genuinely have nothing to cite.
